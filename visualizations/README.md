## Chart Index

### Chart 1 — Sales by Category (Bar Chart)
**File:** `01_sales_by_category.png`  
**Chart Type:** Vertical bar chart  
**Business Question:** Which product category generates the most revenue?

**What it shows:** Total sales revenue aggregated by the three product categories — Technology, Office Supplies, and Furniture. Each bar represents the cumulative sales across all 4 years.

**Business Decision Support:** Category managers can use this to prioritize inventory, marketing spend, and supplier negotiations toward the highest-revenue categories.

---

### Chart 2 — Monthly Sales Trend (Line Chart)
**File:** `02_monthly_sales_trend.png`  
**Chart Type:** Line chart with area fill  
**Business Question:** How does sales volume change month-over-month?

**What it shows:** Monthly aggregated sales from January 2020 to December 2023. The shaded area beneath the line emphasizes volume magnitude. The chart captures seasonality, year-over-year patterns, and growth trends.

**Business Decision Support:** Operations and supply chain teams can use this to anticipate demand spikes (Q4 holiday season) and plan inventory accordingly.

---

### Chart 3 — Profit by Region (Horizontal Bar Chart)
**File:** `03_profit_by_region.png`  
**Chart Type:** Horizontal bar chart  
**Business Question:** Which geographic region is most profitable?

**What it shows:** Total net profit by US region (East, West, Central, South). Bars are colored green for positive profit regions and red for any loss-making region. A reference line at zero marks the break-even point.

**Business Decision Support:** Regional sales directors can use this to identify underperforming areas and allocate resources to the most profitable markets.

---

### Chart 4 — Sales by Customer Segment (Donut + Bar)
**File:** `04_sales_by_segment.png`  
**Chart Type:** Combination — Donut chart (share) + Bar chart (absolute value)  
**Business Question:** How is revenue distributed across customer segments?

**What it shows:** A donut chart on the left shows each segment's percentage share of total sales. A bar chart on the right shows absolute sales values for Consumer, Corporate, and Home Office segments.

**Business Decision Support:** Marketing teams can use this to understand which segments to prioritize for growth campaigns vs. which to retain with loyalty programs.

---

### Chart 5 — Discount vs. Profit (Scatter Plot)
**File:** `05_discount_vs_profit.png`  
**Chart Type:** Scatter plot with regression trend line  
**Business Question:** Does giving discounts reduce profitability?

**What it shows:** Each point represents one transaction, plotted against its discount rate (X-axis) and resulting profit (Y-axis). Points are color-coded by category. A red dashed trend line shows the overall direction of the relationship. The Pearson correlation coefficient is displayed.

**Business Decision Support:** Pricing and finance teams can use this to establish discount guardrails that prevent transactions from becoming loss-making. The strong negative correlation confirms that high discounts destroy margins.

---

### Chart 6 — Correlation Heatmap
**File:** `06_correlation_heatmap.png`  
**Chart Type:** Annotated heatmap  
**Business Question:** How do numerical variables relate to each other?

**What it shows:** A symmetric correlation matrix showing Pearson correlation coefficients between Sales, Profit, Quantity, Discount, and Profit Margin %. Color scale: green = strong positive, red = strong negative, white = no correlation. Values are annotated in each cell.

**Business Decision Support:** Analysts can use this to quickly identify which variables are worth investigating together and which are statistically independent.

**Key Correlations Found:**
- Sales ↔ Profit: **+0.785** (strong positive — more sales = more profit)
- Discount ↔ Profit Margin %: **-0.854** (strong negative — discounts destroy margins)
- Discount ↔ Profit: **-0.259** (moderate negative)

### Chart 7 — Top 10 Products by Sales (Horizontal Bar Chart)
**File:** `07_top10_products.png`  
**Chart Type:** Horizontal bar chart  
**Business Question:** Which individual products drive the most revenue?

**What it shows:** The 10 highest-revenue products, sorted by total sales in descending order. Color intensity increases with sales rank.

**Business Decision Support:** Product managers can use this to identify flagship products worth protecting (pricing, availability, promotions) and to understand which SKUs drive portfolio revenue.

---

### Chart 8 — Sales and Profit by Sub-Category (Comparative Bar)
**File:** `08_sales_profit_subcategory.png`  
**Chart Type:** Grouped horizontal bar chart  
**Business Question:** Which sub-categories are high-revenue but low-profit, and which are genuinely profitable?

**What it shows:** For each of the 17 sub-categories, two bars appear — one for total sales (blue) and one for total profit (green). Profit bars are colored red where profit is negative. This creates an immediate visual comparison between revenue-generating and profit-generating sub-categories.

**Business Decision Support:** Category managers can identify sub-categories that generate revenue but destroy profit (high sales, negative profit) — these require immediate pricing or discount policy intervention.

---

### Chart 9 — Year-over-Year Comparison (Multi-panel Bar)
**File:** `09_yearly_comparison.png`  
**Chart Type:** Three panel bar charts  
**Business Question:** How has the business performed year over year?

**What it shows:** Three side-by-side bar charts comparing 2020, 2021, 2022, and 2023 performance across Total Sales, Total Profit, and Unique Orders. Allows rapid year-over-year comparison.

**Business Decision Support:** Executive leadership can use this for annual performance reviews, target setting, and investment planning.

---

### Chart 10 — Profit Distribution by Category (Box Plot)
**File:** `10_profit_distribution.png`  
**Chart Type:** Box plot  
**Business Question:** What is the spread and distribution of profit per transaction within each category?

**What it shows:** For each category, a box shows the 25th–75th percentile range (IQR), the median line, whiskers extending to 1.5×IQR, and individual outlier points beyond. A red dashed line marks the zero-profit break-even threshold.

**Business Decision Support:** Risk managers and category analysts can use this to understand the "tail risk" of each category — how often do transactions fall below zero? How extreme are the outliers?
